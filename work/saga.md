Why you didn’t hear about Fables
Steven Pressfield talks about combating resistance.

The more important a call to action is to our soul's evolution, the more Resistance we will feel about answering it

Many founders feel this. That Resistance can feel insurmountable. I’m no different. I wanted Fables to be perfect. So we kept working on it in stealth, we built many prototypes, explored the problems in TTRPG’s that matter to Fables. We tested and tweaked and tweened. We took our time to get it right

We were ducks, calm on top, going wild underneath the water.

Then we started to see Fables everywhere.


Live reaction of us seeing all the fables out there
…and we took it personally.

While we took look passed the mushroom company fable (shout out to the amazing storyteller Jim Fuller who is a Co-Founder), the name in the coffin was Fablecraft (and now fable the game is set to return in 2024).

So around the start year we paid our respects to Fables and worked on what would come next.

Why Saga?
While Fables felt like a great name, and the logo really stuck in our mind. It did have some holes that were overlooked. The most famous use of Fables, is Aesop’s Fables, especially the Tortoise and the Hare. Fables are moral stories that use of Anthropomorphism to better illustrate that moral.

That’s not really what we are trying to do.

Then the runic F, ᚠ ? That is Fehu, which means more mobile wealth or cattle. It’s a bit of a reach. Looks like an F but the meaning doesn’t adhere to the values of storytelling.

A closer rune would be ᚩ or ᚨ which would mean mouth or god. A much better meaning but doesn’t really evoke the pen/quill motiff.


We did try
Changing a name is a lot easier when you have your brand set up. Visual Identity is just the visual manifestation of the brand, not the whole thing. So taking Fables and the logo mark away was a smaller pivot than anticipated.

Saga came from our purpose. Saga’s toolkit can work great for a single 3-4 hours session of your favourite TTRPG but it thrives on long form storytelling. A saga is a long form account of events.

Don’t discount mouthfeel
Say the name of your business/startup. Say it again. Repeat it again and again and pay attention to how it sounds when it leaves your mouth. This might seem strange at first but the mouthfeel of your brand is important. We spent a good amount of time trialing the mouthfeel of Sagas and Saga (We went down a path of palindromes and the mouthfeel helped decide).

The hunt for the new Logo
A brand always evolves, it shouldn’t remain static. But you needed to place your flag to begin.

At different fidelities we played with different treatments. They ranged from reusing the runic f to look like an s, to a very simple dice logo and to getting into the speech bubbles and quote marks. TTRPG’s express themselves through the spoken word of many.


A slew of different treatments.
We played with runic S but the convergence between written and spoken stories stuck with us. Scrolls with the S instead or a scroll in the shape of an S was played with.


So we kept going with the intersection of speech bubble and scroll and increased fidelity.


The scroll used to be long, which worked with the S inside but didn’t mean much. So we dropped the S and shrank the scroll bubble to fit cleanly in a square.


The Scroll Bubble felt like the right place to take Saga,
- it was the convergence of spoken and written storytelling
- it merged old and new
- it worked for both digital products and physical book spines


The current Saga Logo
The Blackletter S that was housed inside the long Scroll Bubble was moved out and gave us the font style for the word mark of Saga. We played with sans-serif usage but it didn’t evoke the long form storytelling that Saga spoke to.

We’re no strangers to brand refreshes
Last year we introduced a new brand for Storytale, we changed the visual identity. This year we had to change Fables to Saga. Both were for different reasons.

The waveform logo for storytale was created at the start of our journey and a year in it didn’t speak to the studio we had become. We updated the values of Storytale and the visuals needed to be updated with it.

Fables needed to change due to the market changes. We needed to stand out against our competitors. We had the brand values firmly established but needed to change the name and visual identity to better hold a desired market relevance.

Will this be what Saga will always look like? It probably shouldn’t be. We know from experience that once you release your startup to the world things start to change.

When we Saga goes out of beta and in full release, you’ll likely see updates to the brand. So far the story of Saga has been insulated from the outside world. But great stories are told together and it will change when you start to use it and shape how you can be the best storyteller you can.
